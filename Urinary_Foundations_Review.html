Section A

Two Systems, One Job

One system makes urine. The other moves it out. Know which is which.

Kidneys vs. Urinary System

Kidneys — nephrologist

  • Make urine → control fluid volume
  • Regulate electrolytes
  • Help control blood pressure
  • Remove metabolic waste (BUN, creatinine, uric acid) + drugs
  • Convert vitamin D (for Ca⁺⁺)
  • Acid–base: make, retain, excrete HCO₃⁻
  • Make erythropoietin

Urinary system — urologist

  • Transports urine (ureters)
  • Stores urine (bladder)
  • Eliminates urine (urethra)
Acids: volatile acid = CO₂ (a gas → lungs). Nonvolatile acids are dissolved in fluid → must leave in urine.
Core Concept

Only ½ a Working Kidney Keeps You Fine

So kidney damage hides until a LOT of nephrons are gone. Artificial kidney = dialysis.
Blood Supply

Blood Flow To & From the Kidneys

  • Renal artery off the aorta brings oxygenated blood in
  • Renal vein drains into the inferior vena cava
  • No flow, no filtering
Cardiac output ↓ → kidney blood flow ↓ → renal function is impaired quickly.
Clinical Pearl

¼ of Cardiac Output Goes to the Kidneys

That’s why the kidneys are among the first organs to suffer when the heart pumps less (spiral: Ch 4 Cardiovascular).

The Urethra: Short vs. Long

Female — about 1.5 inches

  • Short path for bacteria to reach the bladder
  • Plus sitting to void
  • ↑ UTI risk

Male — 6 to 8 inches

  • Longer path
  • Runs through the prostate, just below the bladder
  • Prostate problems can squeeze it (BPH — Disorders deck)
Voiding

Bladder Full → Brain Says Go

  • Normal capacity: about 200–300 mL (about 1 cup)
  • Pressure on sphincters + stretch receptors → brain → urge to void
  • Bladder contracts + external sphincter relaxes → urine out
  • Urination (micturition) is voluntary
  • Daytime control around 3 years (USA): feel it, communicate it, relax
Red Flag

At Least 30 mL per Hour

Critically ill patients get urine measured hourly. Normal daily output for a healthy adult: about 1,500–2,000 mL / 24 hr (lecture: up to 2,500, depending on intake).
Quick Check
An ICU client after a large GI bleed has a BP of 82/50. Urine output the last 2 hours: 18 mL, then 15 mL. Best explanation?
A. Weak bladder sphincters are leaking urine around the catheter
No — this is a making-urine problem, not a holding-urine problem.
B. Low BP and low blood volume are reducing filtration
Correct. Filtration depends on pressure + volume. ↓ BP and ↓ blood volume → ↓ filtrate → ↓ urine. Under 30 mL/hr is a warning.
C. The Loop of Henle is diluting the urine
Dilute urine means MORE water out, not less.
D. Too much renin is blocking filtration
Renin rises to raise BP and blood volume. It doesn’t block filtration.
Section B

The Nephron

Blood goes in. Most of it comes back. Only a little becomes urine.

The Nephron in 5 Steps

  1. 1Filtration — Bowman’s capsule + glomerulus
  2. 2PCT — reabsorbs ~90% back to the blood
  3. 3Loop of Henle — Na⁺ + H₂O in & out; concentrates urine
  4. 4DCT — secretes waste, balances electrolytes, makes HCO₃⁻
  5. 5Collecting duct — now it’s URINE
Renin — nephron cells release it when BP or BV ↓
EPO — released when O₂ is low → bone marrow → RBCs
ADH → DCT keeps H₂O  ·  Aldosterone → DCT keeps Na⁺ + H₂O, dumps K⁺
Most filtrate never becomes urine. Reabsorbed = back to the blood. Secreted = out of the blood into the filtrate.
Step 1

Filtration Needs Pressure + Volume

What drives it

  • Water + particles move out of the blood
  • ↑ pressure → ↑ filtrate
  • ↓ BP or ↓ blood volume → ↓ filtrate
Filtrate looks like serum: no cells, no bacteria, no protein.
*Sugar passes the filter, but there should NOT be a lot of sugar in the urine. Blood glucose normal → no sugar in urine. Sugar in urine → think diabetes.
Core Concept

GFR 125 mL/min Is Normal. Under 60 = Trouble.

GFR is the best indicator of renal function. Under 60 = about half of normal — the kidneys are no longer keeping homeostasis.
Remember

If It Isn’t Filtered, It Isn’t Removed

Clues of Damage

Protein in the Urine = Glomerular Damage

Protein is too large to enter the filtrate — so if it’s in the urine, the filter is broken.

PCT malfunction is one of the first indicators of renal impairment. If the PCT doesn’t reabsorb, the client loses lots of water + electrolytes.
Quick Check
A client with 20 years of poorly controlled hypertension says her urine looks bubbly. Which part of the nephron is most likely damaged?
A. The glomerulus in Bowman’s capsule
Correct. Bubbly = protein. Protein is too big to filter unless ↑ BP has damaged the glomerular network.
B. The Loop of Henle
The Loop concentrates urine (Na⁺ + H₂O). It doesn’t let protein in.
C. The collecting duct
The collecting duct carries finished urine. Protein gets in at filtration.
D. The ureter
The ureter only transports urine — it isn’t part of the nephron.
Section C

What the Kidneys Make

Four products. When the kidneys fail, each one shows up as its own problem.
Kidney Products

Four Products, Four Failures

R
Renin
  • Released when BP ↓ or blood volume ↓
  • RAAS → ↑ BP and ↑ blood volume
E
Erythropoietin (EPO)
  • Not enough O₂ in blood → marrow makes RBCs
  • Failure → anemia (synthetic EPO can be given)
  • Chronic low PaO₂ → polycythemia
D
Active vitamin D
  • Lets the GI tract absorb Ca⁺⁺
  • Failure → ↓ Ca⁺⁺ → PTH → bones give up Ca⁺⁺
  • Osteoporosis; children may not grow tall
HCO₃
Bicarbonate
  • Made and retained by the kidneys
  • Failure → ↓ HCO₃⁻ → metabolic acidosis
Spiral: emphysema, chronic bronchitis, and high altitude = chronic low PaO₂ → healthy kidneys release EPO → high RBC count (Ch 3 + Ch 5).

Hormones at the DCT

ADH vs. Aldosterone

ADH

  • From the posterior pituitary
  • Released when serum Na⁺ or osmolarity is high (> 300 mOsm)
  • DCT reabsorbs water → less urine

Aldosterone

  • From the adrenal gland
  • DCT reabsorbs Na⁺ (water follows)
  • DCT eliminates K⁺ → serum K⁺ ↓
Labs

Waste Labs: BUN & Creatinine

  • Kidneys not clearing waste → ↑ BUN and ↑ creatinine
  • BUN = urea, end product of protein metabolism → reflects liver and kidney
  • Creatinine comes from muscle contraction, cleared only by the kidneys → more precise
  • A 50% drop in GFR doubles the creatinine
  • BUN rises too, but not an exact double — hydration, protein intake, GI bleeding, and the liver also change it
  • 24-hr urine for creatinine clearance measures GFR
Aging

The Aging Kidney

  • Kidney function decreases with age — worse with ↑ BP and diabetes
  • Waste and water build up in the blood; homeostasis slips
  • Related problems: anemia (↓ EPO), hypertension, osteoporosis (Ca⁺⁺)
  • Drugs the kidneys can’t excrete build up → drug toxicity
Pearl: a drug that is toxic to the kidney is toxic to the ear — they form from the same tissue at the same time in utero.
Urinalysis

Read the Cup: What Abnormal Means

Normal urine: water, electrolytes, waste. No blood, protein, bacteria, casts, ketones, or sugar (if blood glucose is normal). Not cloudy.
Also: glucose → diabetes · ketones / acetone odor → DKA (metabolic acidosis)
Diagnostic Tests

Six Tests — What Each One Shows

1
Urinalysis
  • What’s in the urine
2
Bedside bladder scan
  • Nurse’s ultrasound: how much urine is in the bladder
  • “Feels like I need to go, but can’t”
3
Renal ultrasound
  • Size + shape of kidneys, ureters, bladder
  • Does not show function
4
IVP (intravenous pyelogram)
  • IV dye enters the filtrate; X-ray shows urine flow
  • Dye is toxic · check allergies · bowel prep
5
Cystourethrogram
  • Tube → bladder → dye
  • X-rays while voiding (bladder + voiding capacity)
6
Cystoscopy
  • MD inserts a scope
  • Looks directly at urethra, bladder, ureters
NCLEX Check
A client with long-standing kidney disease has a low hemoglobin. Which kidney function best explains it?
A. Reduced renin release
Renin is about BP and blood volume, not RBCs.
B. Reduced erythropoietin release
Correct. Kidneys release EPO when blood O₂ is low → marrow makes RBCs. Impaired kidneys → ↓ EPO → anemia.
C. Reduced vitamin D activation
Vitamin D affects Ca⁺⁺ and bone, not hemoglobin.
D. Reduced response to ADH
ADH controls water reabsorption at the DCT.
Big Picture

Urinary Foundations — The Big Picture

• Kidneys make urine; the urinary system moves it (transport → store → eliminate).
• Kidneys get ¼ of cardiac output. Critically ill: at least 30 mL/hr.
• Filtration needs pressure + volume. Filtrate looks like serum — no cells, no protein.
• GFR 125 normal; under 60 = out of homeostasis. Not filtered = not removed.
• Four products, four failures: renin (BP), EPO (anemia), vitamin D (bone), HCO₃⁻ (acidosis).
• Bubbly = protein; cloudy = bacteria.
