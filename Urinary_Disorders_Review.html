Section A

Incontinence

Loss of urinary control — and what it costs the client.
Core Concept

Stress Incontinence Is the Most Common

  • ↑ Intra-abdominal pressure — cough, sneeze, laugh, lift, exercise
  • Pushes on a weakened sphincter → urine leaks
  • A short female urethra makes it worse
Stress Incontinence

What Weakens the Sphincter?

♀
Pelvic changes
  • Pregnancy, childbirth
  • Menopause
↓
Cystocele
  • Anatomy change: bladder hangs into the vagina
↑
More pressure
  • Obesity
  • Chronic coughing
♂
Prostate removal
  • Surgery near the sphincter
Risk factors for any incontinence: female, advancing age, overweight, smoking, other diseases.
Types

The Other Types

Urge / overactive bladder
  • Sudden, intense urge → involuntary loss
  • UTI, caffeine, nicotine, constipation, Parkinson’s, stroke
Reflex
  • Nervous-system damage
  • Detrusor contracts with no sensation to void — no urgency
Overflow
  • Can’t empty (retention) → dribbling, weak stream
  • Spinal cord injury, blockage, prostate
Functional
  • Physical or mental impairment → can’t get there in time
  • Arthritis, broken hand → elastic-waist pants
Transient
  • Temporary cause: delirium, infection, diuretics, sedatives, constipation, alcohol, caffeine
Enuresis · Mixed · Gross total
  • Enuresis: after age 4–5 (nocturnal = bed-wetting)
  • Mixed: more than one type
  • Gross total: continuous leak, no storage

Urge vs. Overflow

Urge — bladder too eager

  • Sudden, intense urge, then leaks
  • Irritants: UTI, caffeine, nicotine
  • Brain conditions: Parkinson’s, Alzheimer’s, stroke
  • Overactive bladder = urge with no known cause

Overflow — bladder can’t empty

  • Retention → dribbling, weak stream
  • Spinal cord injury → can’t sense the need to void
  • Urethral blockage, prostate conditions
  • Chronic holding it → overstretched detrusor
Clinical Pearl

Post-Void Residual: Under 50 mL

What’s left in the bladder after voiding. Check it with a bedside bladder scan.

Neurogenic Bladder

When the Nerves to the Bladder Are Cut Off

  • Bladder dysfunction from an interruption of normal nerve supply
  • Causes: brain or spinal cord injury, dementia, Parkinson’s, stroke, MS, spina bifida, diabetes, vaginal childbirth, chronic alcoholism
  • Shows symptoms of both an overactive and an underactive bladder
  • Treated by cause, using the incontinence treatments
Spiral: stroke, Parkinson’s, dementia, and spinal cord injury interrupt the brain–bladder signal (Ch 11 Neural).
Quick Check
A 52-year-old post-menopausal client leaks urine when she laughs or sneezes. Which history item is a stress incontinence risk factor?
A. Three vaginal births and a chronic smoker’s cough
Correct — childbirth weakens the sphincter; chronic coughing raises intra-abdominal pressure.
B. Parkinson’s disease
No — Parkinson’s is a cause of urge incontinence.
C. Stroke last year
No — stroke is a cause of urge incontinence.
D. Four cups of coffee a day
No — caffeine is a bladder irritant (urge / overactive bladder).
Section B

Urinary Tract Infection

Lower UTI is a bladder problem. Upper UTI means the kidney is involved.

Bacteria Climb Up the Tract

Bacteria climb against the flow of urine: urethra → bladder → ureter → kidney.
A urinary catheter can carry bacteria in → iatrogenic UTI.
Cystitis

Cystitis = Inflamed Bladder — Who’s at Risk?

♀
Women
  • Urethra within an inch of the anus
  • Not voiding every 3–4 hr (stagnant urine)
  • Sex, tight clothes, perineal irritants
  • Post-menopause, pregnancy, alkaline urine, stones
♂
Men
  • Age
  • Uncircumcised
  • Anal sex
  • Stones, BPH
The bladder and urethra walls turn red and swollen. Cause: infection or irritants.
Cystitis

Signs & Treatment

!
Signs
  • Urgency, frequency
  • Burning, pain, pelvic pressure
  • Hematuria (blood in urine)
  • May have fever, chills
Rx
Treatment
  • Fluids
  • OTC urinary antiseptic (AZO)
  • Pyridium → turns urine orange
  • Maybe antibiotics
  • Cranberry juice acidifies urine
Quick Check
A client taking Pyridium for cystitis calls, worried: “My urine is bright orange.” Best response?
A. “Go to the ER — that’s blood in your urine.”
Hematuria looks red, not bright orange. Orange is the expected drug effect.
B. “That’s expected with Pyridium.”
Correct. Pyridium makes pee orange. It eases symptoms — tell the provider if fever or back pain appears.
C. “Your kidneys are failing.”
Orange color alone isn’t a sign of kidney failure.
D. “You’re dehydrated — stop the drug.”
Dehydration makes urine dark yellow; the orange is the drug.
Very Serious

Back Pain + Fever > 101°F = Pyelonephritis

The UTI has reached one or both kidneys. Pain on a thump to the back (flank), high fever, ↑ WBCs. Needs IV antibiotics in the hospital — often 4–6 weeks.

How Pyelonephritis Damages the Kidney

Complications: renal failure, recurrent UTIs, sepsis.

Cystitis vs. Pyelonephritis

Cystitis — lower UTI

  • Where: bladder
  • Signs: urgency, frequency, burning, pelvic pressure, hematuria
  • Urine: cloudy, foul-smelling, WBCs
  • Tx: fluids, AZO / Pyridium, maybe antibiotics

Pyelonephritis — upper UTI

  • Where: kidney(s)
  • Signs: severe UTI signs + flank/back pain, fever > 101°F, malaise, ↑ BP
  • Labs: ↑ WBC; urine + blood cultures, CBC
  • Tx: IV antibiotics in hospital, 4–6 wk
NCLEX Check
Which finding best tells the nurse that a client’s UTI has moved from the bladder to the kidney?
A. Burning and frequency on urination
Happens with any UTI, including cystitis.
B. Cloudy urine with a foul odor
Points to a UTI, but doesn’t tell you where.
C. Flank pain with a temperature of 102°F
Correct. Back/flank pain + fever over 101°F = pyelonephritis.
D. Hematuria
Blood in the urine is a general UTI sign.
Section C

Kidney Stones

A ureter about as wide as a pencil eraser.
Nephrolithiasis

A Stone Can Plug the Ureter

  • Hard mineral crystals the kidneys normally excrete — in the renal pelvis, ureters, or bladder
  • Most common: calcium + oxalate or phosphate (also struvite/infection, uric acid, cystine)
  • Stones are a common cause of UTI; more common in men
  • Risks: pH changes, concentrated salts, urinary stasis, family history, obesity, HTN, diet
Section D

Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

Not cancer — but it squeezes the urethra.
The Prostate

Where It Sits Explains Everything

  • Sits just below the bladder
  • Surrounds the urethra at the base of the bladder
  • Lies in front of the rectum → felt with a digital rectal exam
  • Male reproductive tract: the ductus deferens and seminal vesicles join at the prostate
So: anything that enlarges the prostate squeezes the urethra and blocks urine leaving the bladder.
Core Concept

BPH = Hyperplasia, Not Cancer

Non-malignant enlargement with aging. Cause unknown — declining testosterone + ↑ estrogen may drive stromal cells to multiply, or prostate stem cells fail to mature and die on schedule. (Spiral: Ch 1 cell adaptation — hyperplasia = more cells.)

From Big Prostate to Kidney Damage — read from the bottom up

BPH

Signs, Risk, Diagnosis, Treatment

!
Signs
  • Can’t start peeing, weak stream, dribbling
  • Frequency, urgency, nocturia
  • Retention, overflow incontinence, UTI
  • Erectile dysfunction
45
Risk
  • Age over 45
Dx
Diagnosis
  • Digital rectal exam
  • PSA — elevated with cancer
  • Cancer feels hard, nodular, doesn’t move
  • Urine flow, urinalysis, ultrasound, cystoscopy
Rx
Treatment
  • Saw palmetto, lycopene (red foods)
  • Avoid alcohol
  • Alpha-blockers, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors
  • TURP: transurethral resection of the prostate
NCLEX Check
A 70-year-old man with BPH has his third UTI this year. Which explanation links the two?
A. BPH is a cancer that spreads to the bladder lining
BPH is benign — not cancer.
B. The prostate lengthens the urethra, so bacteria travel farther
A longer path would lower risk, not raise it.
C. The prostate squeezes the urethra, so urine stays in the bladder and bacteria multiply
Correct. Squeezed urethra → incomplete emptying (↑ post-void residual) → stagnant urine → UTI again and again.
D. Rising testosterone weakens the bladder’s defenses
BPH is linked to declining testosterone.
Section E

Clinical Application

Put it all together
Clinical Scenario
Dana Reyes, 24F, college swimmer. Three days of burning and frequency; she has been taking AZO and her urine is orange. She often “holds it” through long practices and sits in a wet suit for hours. Today: chills and pain in her right lower back. T 102.4°F, HR 108, BP 138/86. Pain when the right flank is tapped. Urine cloudy and foul-smelling with WBCs and blood. CBC: ↑ WBC.
What is happening, and what does Dana need?
A. Cystitis — keep taking AZO and push fluids at home
No. Flank pain + fever over 101°F + ↑ WBC means the infection has climbed to the kidney. AZO only eases symptoms.
B. Pyelonephritis — IV antibiotics in the hospital
Correct. Short urethra + holding urine (stagnant) → cystitis → bacteria climbed to the kidney. Very serious: risk of kidney damage and sepsis.
C. Kidney stone — strain her urine
Stones can block flow, but cloudy urine with WBCs, fever, and three days of burning point to infection that reached the kidney.
D. Stress incontinence from swim training
Stress incontinence is leaking with pressure — no fever, no WBCs.
Clinical Scenario
Walter Okafor, 74M, retired bus driver. Up 4 times a night to void, hard to start the stream, dribbling. Second UTI in 6 months. Bladder scan after voiding: 320 mL left. Digital rectal exam: enlarged, smooth prostate that moves. BUN and creatinine mildly ↑. Takes his alpha-blocker “when I remember.”
Which statement best explains Walter’s picture?
A. His post-void residual is normal; the UTIs are unrelated
No — normal is under 50 mL. 320 mL is a lot of stagnant urine.
B. The prostate findings point to cancer
Cancer feels hard, nodular, and fixed. His is smooth and moves — fits BPH.
C. BPH is squeezing his urethra → retention → recurrent UTI, with early back-pressure on the kidneys
Correct. Squeezed urethra → hesitancy, dribbling, nocturia, 320 mL residual → stagnant urine → UTIs. Rising BUN/creatinine = back-pressure (hydroureter) starting to hurt kidney function.
D. He has urge incontinence from an overactive bladder
His problem is emptying, not a sudden urge — that’s retention from obstruction.
Big Picture

Urinary Disorders — The Big Picture

• Stress incontinence (most common) = pressure on a weak sphincter.
• Stagnant urine → UTI. Post-void residual should be under 50 mL.
• Cystitis = bladder. Pyridium turns urine orange.
• Back thump + fever > 101°F = pyelonephritis → IV antibiotics; risk of sepsis.
• A stone can plug a pencil-eraser-wide ureter.
• BPH is not cancer — it squeezes the urethra → retention → recurrent UTI.
